# Canonical concept

## Name

Working name: **Evidence Gym**. Final brand can change without changing the thesis.

## One sentence

Evidence Gym is a game that trains young people to verify evidence before trusting or sharing content in the AI age.

## Problem

People meet viral claims, real media in false contexts, synthetic content, and fluent AI answers faster than fact-checkers can publish verdicts. A detector may fail on the next generator, and a binary label can make users dependent on another authority. Students need a repeatable verification reflex that still works when the app is closed.

## Solution

The learner becomes an investigator. In short missions they:

1. pause and predict `Trust / Suspicious / Investigate`;
2. state confidence;
3. spend evidence actions on source, date, context, corroboration, provenance, or citation lookup;
4. receive Socratic hints, not a verdict;
5. conclude on three independent axes;
6. update confidence and choose whether/how to share;
7. receive an Evidence Receipt and targeted future practice.

## Signature model

| Axis | Question | Example outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Media authenticity | Is the artifact synthetic, altered, authentic, or unknown? | Authentic |
| Claim veracity | What do current reliable sources support? | Contradicted |
| Context integrity | Are time, place, author, caption, and framing accurate? | Misleading |

The product deliberately teaches that synthetic does not mean false and authentic does not mean truthful.

## Primary audience

Students and young adults aged 16–24, initially Ukrainian- and English-speaking. Teachers and youth organizations are distribution partners, not a second MVP user experience.

## Differentiators

- XP for investigation quality, not only final answers;
- confidence calibration and rewards for justified mind changes;
- `Insufficient evidence` as a valid result;
- Socratic AI bounded by curated evidence;
- academic citation verification;
- provenance/Content Credentials literacy;
- spaced boosters and unseen transfer tests;
- reviewed, versioned scenario packs.

## Product principles

- Agency over authority.
- Evidence over fluency.
- Process over guessing.
- Calibration over certainty theatre.
- Context over pixels.
- Learning value over engagement at any cost.
- Inclusion and privacy by design.
