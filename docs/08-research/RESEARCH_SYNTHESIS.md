# Research synthesis

## Evidence-supported foundations

- Lateral reading/source tracing is a defensible behavioral backbone; use Stanford Civic Online Reasoning material as primary reference.
- Inoculation/prebunking studies support training manipulation recognition, while effect size/durability depend on intervention and boosters.
- Spaced retrieval is established learning science; adapting FSRS to MIL reasoning remains a testable hypothesis.
- C2PA/Content Credentials can support provenance literacy but do not prove truth or absence of manipulation.
- Ukrainian Learn to Discern work supports the relevance and measurability of MIL training; it does not prove Evidence Gym effectiveness.
- Deepfake robustness work supports “detector as one clue,” not detector-as-verdict.

## Product hypotheses, not established facts

Three-axis judgment improves reasoning; Socratic AI improves transfer vs direct answers; Evidence Process Rubric is reliable/valid; attention-budget mechanics improve search; FSRS scheduling improves MIL retention. Label and test these explicitly.

## Recommended experiments

1. Socratic hints vs direct-answer AI vs no AI; unseen no-AI transfer after 7 days.
2. Three axes vs binary outcome; errors `synthetic ⇒ false` and `authentic ⇒ true`.
3. Fixed vs adaptive boosters at D7/D30.
4. Provenance lesson with signed synthetic truthful, unsigned authentic and metadata-stripped cases.
5. Academic checker adversarial suite including DataCite DOI, mismatch, retraction, outage and `cannot determine`.

## Measurement

Balanced discernment, source tracing, independent corroboration, context accuracy, Brier/ECE calibration, justified updating, `insufficient evidence` precision/recall, true/false share rates, D7/D30 retention, unseen transfer, evidence efficiency and inter-rater reliability of process rubric.

## Evidence hierarchy

A: official rules/standards, primary peer-reviewed research and first-party technical docs. B: reputable secondary synthesis/maintainer docs. C: discovery/community/AI-generated notes. Product claims cite A where possible and distinguish observed, inferred, hypothesized and target.
