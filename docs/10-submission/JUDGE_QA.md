# Judge Q&A bank

Use this bank for adversarial rehearsal. Answers must match the current prototype and evidence appendix.

## Product and learning

### Is this just another fake-news quiz?

No. The scored unit is the learner’s process: source inspection, evidence selection, context checks, and confidence calibration. Scenarios may contain false content, authentic content in false context, or insufficient evidence.

### Why not use an AI detector?

Detectors can be a signal, but they are not a durable curriculum or a universal source of truth. Evidence Gym teaches behaviours that still work when the format, platform, or generation method changes.

### How do you know learning occurred?

We separate usability, short-term task performance, and durable transfer. [Insert completed evidence.] Claims about long-term impact remain hypotheses until measured with delayed or transfer tasks and an appropriate comparison.

### What prevents users from gaming the score?

The score favours investigation steps and evidence quality over a binary answer. Repeated content and exposed answer patterns must be controlled during formal evaluation.

## AI and trust

### Can the AI hallucinate feedback?

The intended design constrains feedback to the reviewed scenario evidence, requires citations, records uncertainty, and provides a safe fallback. Generated output is never treated as ground truth merely because a model produced it.

### What if the correct answer changes?

Scenario packs are versioned, time-bounded, and reviewable. A correction process can withdraw or replace a scenario without rewriting historical learner records.

### Is the product politically neutral?

No learning product is free of editorial choices. We aim for transparent, consistent selection criteria, evidence provenance, viewpoint diversity where relevant, and an appeals/correction path—not an unsupported claim of perfect neutrality.

## Safety and inclusion

### How do you protect minors?

The operating model uses data minimisation, age-aware consent, no unnecessary profiling, reviewed content, reporting and escalation paths, and a separate legal/safeguarding review before real-world deployment with minors.

### Will the product expose harmful misinformation?

Scenarios are curated and framed for learning, with content warnings and amplification controls. High-risk content can be excluded, redacted, or restricted to supervised settings.

### What about low-connectivity users?

The product plans compact, cacheable scenario content and progressive enhancement. The submission must state which offline or low-bandwidth behaviours are actually implemented.

## Feasibility and scale

### Can four people maintain this?

The initial model limits scope: a small reviewed content set, a modular product, explicit contracts, and external partnerships for subject-matter review and distribution. Scale is based on governed scenario packs, not unlimited AI generation.

### What is the biggest dependency?

High-quality, rights-cleared, reviewed scenarios and pilot access are more critical than any single model provider. Both require partner processes and documented fallback plans.

### What happens after the hackathon?

Use the 30/60/90-day roadmap: validate the core loop, complete a supervised pilot, publish the correction and contributor workflow, then expand only when quality and safety gates pass.

### What is your sustainability model?

The current strategy combines grants/partnerships for public-interest access with optional institutional services. Any revenue model must preserve learner privacy and must not depend on selling behavioural data.
