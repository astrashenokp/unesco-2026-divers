# Research data management plan

Complete this plan before collecting participant data.

## Data inventory

| Data element | Why necessary | Source | Identifiability | Access group | Storage | Retention/deletion | Consent/legal basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Participant code | link session artefacts without name | study process | pseudonymous | [group] | [approved location] | [date/rule] | [basis] |

If a field has no decision-changing purpose, do not collect it.

## Separation

- Store contact/consent records separately from observation and task data.
- Use a random participant code, not initials, school number, email, or birth date.
- Keep the re-identification key only when operationally necessary, access-limited, and time-bounded.
- Do not place research data in issue trackers, public repositories, shared design files, prompts, or screenshots.

## Recording and transcription

Record only the modes explicitly consented to. Define who can download, transcribe, redact, and delete recordings. Review vendor terms and data location before automated transcription. Treat transcripts as identifiable until proven otherwise.

## Access and transfer

Document named access groups, least privilege, authentication, secure transfer, access review dates, partner data roles, and what happens when a collaborator leaves. Email attachments and public links are not an approved research store.

## Retention and deletion

Define separate schedules for contact details, consent evidence, raw recordings, notes, analysed data, and reports. Verify deletion from local devices, exports, vendor systems, and backups to the extent promised. Record completion.

## Publication and sharing

- Publish aggregate or carefully anonymised results only within consent scope.
- Review small cells, rare attributes, free text, quotations, images, and combinations that permit re-identification.
- A pseudonymous dataset is still personal data when a key or reasonable linkage exists.
- Open-data release requires a separate necessity, consent, rights, and re-identification assessment.

## Incident and withdrawal

Define the private incident route, containment steps, notification decision, and withdrawal lookup process. If deletion can no longer reach already anonymised or published aggregates, explain that limitation before consent.
