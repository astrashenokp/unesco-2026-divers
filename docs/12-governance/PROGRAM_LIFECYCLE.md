# Programme lifecycle: idea to responsible sunset

This lifecycle joins product, evidence, content, partnerships, and operations. It does not prescribe implementation ownership.

## Stage 0 — mandate and constraints

Outputs:

- submission/initiative rules captured from the authoritative source;
- target audience and jurisdiction hypothesis;
- team IP/authority understood;
- initial safety, rights, privacy, accessibility, cost, and deadline constraints;
- a list of claims that still require verification.

Exit: the initiative is eligible, ethically plausible, and small enough to test.

## Stage 1 — problem discovery

Outputs:

- interviews or secondary evidence about real verification behaviour;
- personas treated as hypotheses until evidenced;
- prioritised user problem and non-goals;
- decision on who should not be served yet;
- assumptions and open questions register.

Exit: a concrete behaviour problem is evidenced without pitching the solution as the evidence.

## Stage 2 — concept and learning design

Outputs:

- canonical concept and learning loop;
- learning outcomes and assessment rubric;
- safe scenario-authoring method;
- value proposition and differentiation;
- prototype/pilot success and stop thresholds.

Exit: the team can test the riskiest assumption with a bounded experience.

## Stage 3 — prototype and content proof

Outputs:

- two end-to-end scenarios, including authentic media in misleading context;
- provenance, rights, accessibility, and content review records;
- deterministic demo path and limitations;
- corrected public claims and evidence appendix.

Exit: a representative user can experience the learning loop safely enough for supervised testing.

## Stage 4 — usability validation

Outputs:

- reviewed consent/research protocol;
- observation data with minimum personal information;
- critical usability, safety, and accessibility findings;
- decisions tied to evidence;
- revised prototype/content versions.

Exit: predeclared usability and safety thresholds pass, or the concept returns for revision.

## Stage 5 — learning pilot

Outputs:

- immediate-learning and transfer measures appropriate to the sample;
- partner/facilitator operating brief;
- completed pilot report with limitations;
- content correction and incident evidence;
- an explicit proceed/revise/stop decision.

Exit: there is enough evidence for a narrow public or partner beta claim—never more.

## Stage 6 — launch readiness

Outputs:

- privacy/legal/vendor/license/rights decisions;
- public terms/information, reporting, correction, deletion, and support paths;
- cost/runway case and dependency fallbacks;
- release, incident, moderation, and content-expiry readiness;
- signed partner boundaries and brand permissions.

Exit: every high-risk open question has an accepted decision or blocks launch.

## Stage 7 — measured operation

Outputs:

- metrics with denominators and counter-metrics;
- regular content expiry/correction review;
- incident, accessibility, cost, and partner health reviews;
- controlled experiments with learning and safety guardrails;
- public claims updated when evidence changes.

Exit: continue while outcomes justify the burden and safeguards remain effective.

## Stage 8 — scale or focus

Scale only after a measured bottleneck or repeatable partner demand. Record what standardises, what remains locally reviewed, capacity/cost thresholds, equity effects, and the rollback plan.

## Stage 9 — sunset or transfer

Outputs:

- advance notice appropriate to affected learners/partners;
- export/continuity options where promised;
- data deletion or lawful retention completion;
- revoked credentials and ended vendor/partner access;
- archived scenario provenance and correction history;
- final impact/limitations report and ownership transfer record if applicable.

A dormant service with forgotten data and dependencies is not a completed sunset.
