# Facilitator guide

## Learning posture

The goal is not to expose who is “bad at spotting fakes”. The goal is to make investigation steps visible, normalise uncertainty, and reward evidence-based revision.

## Before the session

- Review every scenario, source, warning, and correct/acceptable reasoning path.
- Confirm audience suitability, accessibility needs, device/network fallback, consent, and local safeguarding process.
- Prepare non-identifiable participant codes if measurement is approved.
- Ensure learners can stop or opt out without public explanation.
- Do not surprise participants with graphic, hateful, targeted, or personally relevant misinformation.

## Facilitation moves

Use questions rather than verdicts:

- “What is the exact claim?”
- “Which evidence would change your confidence?”
- “Is this source independent or repeating another source?”
- “What do we know about date and context?”
- “What remains unknown?”
- “How could we share this conclusion without amplifying harm?”

Praise a justified reduction in confidence. Ask high-confidence learners what could falsify their position. Do not equate speed or assertiveness with skill.

## Handling disagreement

Separate claims, evidence, interpretation, and values. Ask participants to cite the scenario evidence rather than speculate about one another’s motives. When evidence is insufficient, “not enough evidence yet” is a legitimate conclusion.

## Safety response

Pause when a participant is distressed, identifiable private material appears, discussion targets a person/group, or the scenario is factually/technically broken. Follow local escalation and reporting procedure; do not investigate sensitive disclosures in the group.

## After the session

- Debrief the manipulation method without repeating harmful claims unnecessarily.
- Explain limitations of any AI/tool output.
- Capture issues and corrections by scenario version.
- Delete temporary participant data on schedule.
- Report what changed in the programme, not who gave the “wrong” answer.
